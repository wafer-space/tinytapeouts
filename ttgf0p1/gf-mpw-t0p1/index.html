Tiny Tapeout GF 0.1Made on a GlobalFoundries private MPW run

Tiny Tapeout's first, experimental GF180MCU chip. Instead of tiles, its 24 projects, all from earlier Tiny Tapeout chips, were built together into one shared block. Eight small blocks beside it test standard cell libraries from GlobalFoundries, Avalon Semiconductors and OSU on this process.

Data and method

The die and its blocks are measured from the public layout; each project's share of the shared block is counted from that block's netlist.

  • Logic cells are standard cell instances, excluding fillers, taps, antenna diodes and tie cells.
  • Transistors are places where gate polysilicon crosses diffusion, not counting dummy fill.
  • Core area is the whole block.
  • Bond pads are the pad openings in the pad ring, or in a second ring of pads inside it for chip-on-board bonding; probe pads are the others, next to the blocks they test. Letters and logos on the pad layer are not pads.
  • Theoretical maximum tiles one cell at its placement boundary with no routing.
  • Densest 0.1 mm square is the best 0.1 mm square anywhere in each die, sliding in 20 µm steps and avoiding SRAM, given per mm².

What is Tiny Tapeout?

Tiny Tapeout is the quickest and cheapest way to get your own design made on a real chip.

Many designs share one Tiny Tapeout chip. Each project gets one or more small tiles, and a multiplexer on the chip connects the one you choose to the pins. Draw your first design in the browser with no experience by following the digital design guide, or write it in Verilog or another HDL; Tiny Tapeout's templates build and check it on GitHub. The chip comes on a demo board that selects your design and drives it.

Tiny Tapeout also runs workshops and supports teaching in universities and schools. Browse every Tiny Tapeout chip and the projects proven in silicon, or read the Tiny Tapeout FAQ.

Shuttle repository on GitHub

Tiny Tapeout and wafer.space are independent companies that work together (announcement). Tiny Tapeout runs its own shuttles: it takes the projects, builds the die, and sells and supports the chips and boards. wafer.space has the dies manufactured on its GF180MCU runs, and these pages are wafer.space's measurements of them. For anything about a Tiny Tapeout project, chip or order, contact Tiny Tapeout.
